# External stock-video diagnostic — 6 November 2026

The user tested an external Pixabay clip with the saved baseline. The model returned **FAKE with a visual score of 0.9939677119**. Repeating inference reproduced that score exactly. This records a likely false alarm and a generalization concern; the clip's authenticity has not been independently verified, so it is not assigned a confirmed REAL/FAKE label or included in accuracy calculations.

- Reproduced output: `complete`, `visual_only`, predicted label `FAKE`; audio was not analyzed.

## Inspection and interpretation

All 12 face crops were reviewed locally. They contain the intended face. A foreground hand partly obscures the face, and the face becomes visibly less sharp as focus shifts toward the hand later in the clip. The first sampled frame already has a score of about 0.950, so the observed later blur cannot by itself explain the entire prediction.

The current `usable` flag means the frame passed face detection, face-size, and dominant-face checks. It does not certify sharpness, visibility, image authenticity, or reliable classification. Blur and occlusion are plausible contributing factors; this inspection does not establish the cause of the high score.

The model was trained on 210 analyzable videos from a small DFDC sample. Its image encoder was not fine-tuned to learn deepfake-specific features. A high score on this external clip is therefore a concern about generalization and confidence interpretation, rather than evidence that the stock footage was manipulated.

In the recorded held-out DFDC experiment, **4 of 15 known-real videos were classified as fake**: an observed real-video false-positive rate of **26.7%**. The reported **91.1% overall accuracy** applies to 79 analyzable videos from that experiment and does not establish accuracy on arbitrary internet videos. See the [original experiment](dfdc_sample_cpu_2026-11-06.md).

The score is not a verified 99.4% probability of manipulation. Probability calibration needs evidence that predicted probabilities agree with observed frequencies on suitable independent labeled data; this model has not established that reliability for external stock videos. See the [scikit-learn calibration guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html).

## Next experiments

1. Collect independent external clips with sufficiently verified REAL/FAKE labels and source records, and measure false alarms separately from missed fakes. Keep this already-inspected example as a diagnostic/development case if it later informs changes.
2. Assess blur and occlusion flags using training/validation data, including their effect on analysis coverage. Accepting fewer frames alone does not establish improved detection accuracy.
3. Compare deepfake-specific training or fine-tuning on more diverse labeled data, then assess score calibration using appropriate independent calibration data.
4. Evaluate a frozen revised method on a fresh held-out set. Preserve the original model, threshold, and published test results as the first baseline.

No training, threshold changes, or alterations to the original test results were made during this diagnostic. The source video, face crops, and detailed local diagnostic files remain outside Git.
